# Sales reply and negotiation playbook

Read this file for quotations, objections, negotiation, trust repair, order closing, or follow-up strategy. It supplements the core drafting standard in `SKILL.md`.

## Stage map

| Stage | Immediate objective | Useful CTA |
|---|---|---|
| New inquiry | Confirm only the missing facts needed for a relevant answer | Ask for quantity, specification, use, or destination port |
| Quote preparation | Present exact scope and reduce comparison ambiguity | Confirm specification or choose a real option |
| Price objection | Diagnose the comparison and protect value | Ask for target, competing specification, or workable reciprocal term |
| Quote viewed, no reply | Make responding easy and add one new reason | Ask whether price, specification, or timing is the blocker |
| Trial order | Reduce perceived risk and lock details | Confirm model, quantity, sample, inspection, or PI |
| Factory visit | Establish agenda and accurate expectations | Confirm date, attendees, products, and inspection priorities |
| Trust complaint | Acknowledge the concrete issue and correct the record | Agree on verification, inspection, documentation, or next call |
| Order close | Remove the final operational blocker | Confirm PI, deposit, production start, or shipping detail |

## Commercial fact sheet

Before drafting any quotation or price reply, normalize:

| Field | Example format |
|---|---|
| Product | [product/model/specification] |
| Quantity | [amount] [unit] |
| Unit price | [currency] [amount]/[unit] |
| Total | Only if supplied or explicitly calculated |
| Incoterm | [confirmed Incoterm] |
| Port | [confirmed loading port] |
| Included | [confirmed scope] |
| Excluded | Freight, customs, installation, unless stated otherwise |
| Validity | Only if confirmed |

Do not silently change a port, currency, unit, quantity, specification, or price formula. If two source documents disagree, flag the conflict before drafting.

## Price objections

Diagnose before choosing the argument:

- Budget ceiling: acknowledge the all-in budget and explore a real scope or term change.
- Different specification: compare material, thickness, density, finish, tolerance, package, warranty, or testing only when documented.
- Landed-cost concern: separate FOB product value from freight, duty, local handling, and installation.
- Negotiation anchor: stay calm, restate the supported value, and offer only authorized movement.
- Trust gap: evidence and inspection matter more than another sales claim.

A concise price-defense message usually follows this order:

1. Recognize the buyer's target or concern without immediately accepting it.
2. State the real gap and whether the target is feasible.
3. Give one to three verified reasons the current offer differs.
4. State the best authorized position or a conditional route.
5. Ask for one decision.

Never say a cheaper product is poor quality without evidence. A safer comparison is: "To compare fairly, let's put the material, thickness, density, finish, tolerance, packing, and warranty side by side."

## Concessions

Use a concession only when the user authorizes it. State both sides clearly:

> If you can confirm [quantity/payment/specification/timing], I can request or offer [specific concession].

Do not claim management approval before it exists. Do not invent a discount ladder. Record prior concessions so the next message does not accidentally concede twice.

## Silence after quotation

Do not guilt the buyer or repeat the entire offer. Use one of these truthful angles:

- Decision simplifier: "Is the main concern price, specification, or timing?"
- Comparison help: offer a line-by-line comparison against the buyer's alternative.
- Risk reduction: offer an available sample, inspection, test document, or video call.
- Timing check: ask whether the project is still active and when purchasing is expected.
- Graceful close: after several useful attempts, ask whether to pause the file.

Only mention raw-material increases, production slots, quotation expiry, or shipping changes when current and confirmed.

## Trust repair

When the buyer alleges misrepresentation or a broken promise:

1. Address the specific allegation directly.
2. Correct inaccurate wording without arguing about the buyer's feelings.
3. State the actual business/manufacturing arrangement at the authorized level of detail.
4. Explain the controls BYFIT genuinely performs: specification, production coordination, inspection, documentation, or after-sales responsibility.
5. Offer verifiable next steps such as inspection scope, production records, sample approval, video call, or visit agenda.

Do not hide a material fact behind vague language, attack the buyer, or pressure them to sign confidentiality documents as a substitute for rebuilding trust.

## Multi-message sequences

Generate a sequence only when requested. Each message needs a distinct job:

1. Clarify or deliver the core answer.
2. Add relevant proof.
3. Reduce a specific risk.
4. Make the decision easier.
5. Close or pause respectfully.

The number of messages and timing depend on deal stage, urgency, channel, and buyer behavior; four or five messages are not mandatory. Do not schedule every follow-up on fixed days without considering weekends, local time, project timing, and prior cadence.

## Natural voice

- Sound like Eric, not a brochure or negotiation textbook.
- Prefer short paragraphs and ordinary words.
- Use contractions naturally in conversational English, but not mechanically in formal disputes or contracts.
- Avoid empty openings such as "I hope this email finds you well."
- Avoid excessive exclamation marks, forced humor, inflated claims, and fake precision.
- Do not use internal labels such as `Price Hunter`, `sunk cost`, `decoy`, or `pain layer` in customer-facing text.
- In WhatsApp and WeChat, one strong message is usually better than a mini-email with headings.

## Final check

- Every number and promise is traceable.
- The response addresses the latest buyer message.
- The strongest argument is relevant to this buyer, not generic.
- The CTA is easy to answer.
- No internal supplier name, private customer detail, or unsupported comparison leaked into the draft.
